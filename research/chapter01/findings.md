## F. Migration definitions, NOM values, ERP vs Census

**F1. NOM definition (12/16 month rule)**
**Source:** Australian Bureau of Statistics — article "Measuring Net Overseas Migration in Australia"
**URL:** https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/measuring-net-overseas-migration-australia
(The article is linked from the current population release: "A summary of Net Overseas Migration (NOM), the key data sources, concepts and revision process … can be found in this article: Measuring Net Overseas Migration in Australia.")
Page states article date 12/12/2024, source "National, state and territory population, June 2024".
**Evidence file:** `raw/abs-nom-article-extract.txt`

Verbatim definition quotes:
- "Net overseas migration (NOM) is a demographic concept which reflects the number of people added to (or subtracted from) the Australian population due to international migration."
- "Finalised NOM estimates are determined based on whether a traveller has spent 12 of the last 16 months in or out of Australia. This is known as the 12/16 month rule."
- "A person who is not already resident in Australia, is added to the estimated resident population (ERP) if they travel to Australia and remain present in Australia for 12 months or more (an overseas migrant arrival). Likewise, an Australian resident is removed from the population if they leave Australia for 12 months or more (an overseas migrant departure)."
- "The 12 months does not have to be continuous and is measured over a 16-month period."
- "Preliminary NOM is revised each quarter for four quarters. … After the fourth revision, NOM is regarded as final as it is based on actual migration outcomes."
- "While natural increase is comparatively stable over time, the level of NOM is more subject to change. As a result, changes in Australia's population growth rate are largely driven by changes in NOM."

Same rule stated in the release methodology — "National, state and territory population methodology, March 2026"
(URL: https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/national-state-and-territory-population-methodology/mar-2026, evidence file `raw/abs-population-methodology-extract.txt`):
- "Under a '12/16 month rule', incoming overseas travellers (who are not currently counted in the population) must be resident in Australia for a total period of 12 months or more, during the 16 month follow-up period to then be added to the estimated resident population."
- "The rule of 12 months out of 16 has applied since September quarter 2006. Prior to that, migration was measured using a continuous 12-month period."

**F3. ERP vs Census counts (distinction as stated by ABS)**
From "National, state and territory population methodology, March 2026":
- "Australia's estimated resident population (ERP) includes all people who usually live in Australia (regardless of nationality, citizenship or visa status), with the exception of people present for foreign consular or diplomatic reasons."
- "The latest ERP is based on adjusted 2021 Census counts, updated with quarterly estimates of births, deaths, overseas and interstate migration."
- "The 30 June ERP in a Census year is calculated by: adjusting Census counts of Australian usual residents to account for people missed or counted twice in the Census (based on Post Enumeration Survey results) … adding usual residents temporarily overseas on Census night; adjusting for births, deaths and migration from 30 June to Census night."
- "Quarterly ERP is calculated by taking the population estimate at the start of the quarter and adding natural increase (births minus deaths), net overseas migration and (in the case of state/territory populations) net interstate migration."
